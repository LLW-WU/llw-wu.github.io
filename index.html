LIU Lingwen

Ph.D. Student  ·  Waseda University

Deep learning for medical image reconstruction — and the clinical intelligence that follows.

I am a Ph.D. student at Waseda University. My current research focuses on deep learning for Magnetic Particle Imaging (MPI) reconstruction — developing neural networks that recover high-quality images from raw MPI measurements, a problem that sits at the boundary of inverse problems, signal processing, and modern deep learning.

Before Waseda, I worked on graph AI for brain network analysis during my M.S. at Northeastern University (China). I built self-supervised graph neural networks that model functional brain connectivity to support more objective diagnosis and more personalized treatment of neurological and psychiatric disorders — working closely with clinical collaborators to validate the results. That experience shaped how I think about the full arc from raw medical measurement to clinical decision, and continues to inform how I approach reconstruction quality in my current work.

My work has appeared at WSDM 2024, DASFAA 2025, Science Bulletin, and IEEE TMI. I also spent four months at Goldwind (Fortune Global 500) building energy forecasting systems that went into production.

Research Projects

Graph-based Brain Network Analysis for Neurological Disease Diagnosis

Nov 2022 – Oct 2023

Northeastern University (China)  ·  University of Alberta  ·  Nanjing Medical University

Can a graph neural network read an fMRI scan the way a clinician does?

Disease signals in the brain are subtle and distributed — no single region tells the full story. I built graph-based deep learning models that capture these distributed functional patterns across the whole brain, achieving diagnostic accuracy above 70%, a threshold that collaborating physicians at Nanjing Medical University recognized as clinically meaningful. This work was published at Computers in Biology and Medicine and presented at WSDM 2024.

Personalized Treatment Prediction for Mental Disorders via Multi-modal Learning

Dec 2023 – June 2025

Northeastern University (China)  ·  Nanjing Medical University

Same diagnosis, different drugs — can we predict who responds to what?

Two patients with the same psychiatric diagnosis can respond oppositely to the same treatment, yet this variability is rarely reflected in clinical decision-making. I build multi-modal models that jointly analyze brain functional signals and behavioral patterns to identify biologically distinct patient subgroups — using clustering, PCA, and t-SNE to make the structure visible to clinicians. The goal is to turn "personalized treatment" from a slogan into a data-driven workflow.

Self-supervised Representation Learning on Complex and Dynamic Networks

May 2023 – June 2025

Northeastern University (China)  ·  Peking University  ·  University of Alberta

Most graph learning methods need labels. What if the graph itself is the teacher?

Labeling nodes in real-world networks is slow and expensive, yet the graph's own structure contains enormous latent information. I design self-supervised methods that use structural and temporal cues — topology, evolution, neighborhood patterns — as free supervision signals. The resulting representations generalize across domains: they surface anomalous financial transactions, uncover latent social communities, and improve brain disease classification without additional annotation. This line of work appeared at DASFAA 2025 and IEEE JBHI.

Early Screening for Cerebral Palsy via Machine Vision and Motion Analysis

Sep 2020 – Jun 2021

Shenyang University of Technology  ·  Shenyang Children's Hospital

Earlier diagnosis, better outcomes — using a camera to catch what eyes might miss.

Cerebral palsy is far more treatable when caught early, but early screening still depends on subjective clinical judgment. I built an RGB-D motion capture system (Intel RealSense D435i) with a custom clinical interface to record infant movement, then trained deep learning models to extract subtle motor features predictive of cerebral palsy. Pediatricians at Shenyang Children's Hospital reviewed the identified features and incorporated them into their early diagnostic protocol.

Measuring Relaxation Response to Virtual Reality via Physiological Signals

Sep 2020 – Jun 2021

Shenyang University of Technology

Does VR actually relax you? We measured it.

VR therapy is often promoted for stress and anxiety, but its physiological effects had not been rigorously characterized. I helped design experiments that collected EDA, EMG, and EEG signals while participants underwent structured VR relaxation scenarios, then used machine learning to identify consistent biomarkers of the relaxation response. The results gave the first empirical grounding for this class of intervention, published at IEEE ISR 2021.

Internship & Visits

Data Science Intern — Goldwind

Oct 2024 – Jan 2025

Goldwind Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Fortune Global 500)  ·  Beijing, China

Predicting energy supply and demand — three real deployments, all within 5% error.

At Goldwind, I built and shipped three forecasting systems: 14-day hourly electricity consumption for a provincial grid (Random Forest + XGBoost), next-day power generation for a campus microgrid under varying weather conditions (XGBoost + Lasso), and 14-day electricity spot price forecasting for a regional market (deep MLP in PyTorch). All three achieved ≤5% prediction error and were integrated into Goldwind's operational pipeline.

Research Visit — AI Lab, Singapore Centre for 3D Printing

Aug 2024

Nanyang Technological University, Singapore

Visited NTU's AI Lab to explore how neural 3D reconstruction (NeRF) can improve precision in additive manufacturing — a problem that sits at the boundary of my medical imaging background and engineering applications. Discussions focused on translating reconstruction accuracy into measurable print quality, and opened avenues for future cross-disciplinary collaboration.
