KEY CONCEPT / AI RESEARCH
Failure-Driven
Model Design

A model-design loop that starts with diagnosing a task-relevant failure, not with adding an arbitrary component.

Guiding question

What exactly does the current representation miss—and what evidence would show the proposed change fixes it?

Methodology

How I approach model development

A practical working framework, not a claim that every project has followed these exact steps.

01

Diagnose

Locate the failed examples, structures, or conditions using task-relevant metrics.

02

Hypothesize

Separate possible data, optimization and representation causes.

03

Intervene

Introduce the smallest change that directly tests the proposed mechanism.

04

Validate

Compare against baselines; run ablations and error-slice analyses.

05

Bound

Report trade-offs, failure cases, and where an improvement does not generalize.

Decision rule

Before designing another attention block or loss, ask: Is the problem in the data, the objective, the representation, or the evaluation? A stronger metric alone is not proof of the proposed mechanism.

Structure-aware MPI vessel reconstruction

Ongoing Ph.D. research at Waseda University. The presentation below describes the research motivation and methodological direction, not a published outcome.

Research in progress

Image Fidelity ≠ Vessel Fidelity

In MPI, frequency-domain measurements are mapped back to a spatial tracer distribution. Image-level reconstruction accuracy does not automatically guarantee preservation of vessel shape, boundaries, or skeleton structure.

My current research explores Structure-Before-Image (SBI) as a structure-guided perspective on reconstruction: learn representations that make vessel-relevant structure more explicit, then use that information during image reconstruction.

Inverse problemsSignal-to-imageStructure guidanceRepresentation learning
Research logic
Observation
Good pixel-level metrics can coexist with important vessel-structure errors.
Hypothesis
Purely image-oriented objectives may underrepresent downstream structural fidelity.
Design direction
Structure-sensitive supervision and representation learning during reconstruction.
Evidence to test
Compare image error with shape, boundary and skeleton measures; validate changes with ablations.

Structure-aware Self-supervised Graph Representation Learning

L Liu, P Cao, G Wen, Z Jia, J Yang, W Li, OR Zaiane

DASFAA 2025 - International Conference on Database Systems for Advanced Applications, 2025

We use motif as a structural unit to capture the topological characteristics of nodes and their neighborhoods, and design a motif-based self-supervised learning objective to learn node representations that preserve the structural semantics of the graph. Thus, providing a more robust and discriminative representation for downstream graph-related tasks, e.g., node classification & brain network analysis.

Divergent neural trajectories in young patients with major depressive episodes: longitudinal subtypes and their predictive value for treatment outcomes

J Duan, G Wen, L Liu, S Gu, J Zhou, J Xu, R Zhu, S Hu, P Cao, F Wang

Science Bulletin, S2095-9273 (25) 00917-X

By using interpretable machine learning models based on sub-networks to analyze longitudinal brain imaging and clinical data from young patients with major depressive episodes, we uncover two distinct neural trajectories that characterize different subtypes of the disorder. These subtypes show divergent patterns of brain connectivity changes over time, which are predictive of their respective treatment responses. Our findings suggest that identifying neural trajectories can inform personalized treatment strategies for depression.

Capturing temporal node evolution via self-supervised learning: A new perspective on dynamic graph learning

L Liu, G Wen, P Cao, J Yang, W Li, OR Zaiane

WSDM 2024 - Proceedings of the 17th ACM International Conference on Web Search and Data Mining

We propose to disentangle the temporal evolution of graph nodes into two orthogonal components: time-invariant component that captures the static properties of nodes, and a time-variant evolution representation that captures the dynamic changes. Thus, providing a more interpretable and effective way to understand dynamic graphs, which is crucial for applications like brain network analysis where understanding brain dynamics is key.
